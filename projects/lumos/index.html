Selected project / Student engineering concept

LUMOS

Exploring a different
path to urban light.

A decentralized urban lighting concept oriented around SDG 7. I led the team exploring a system that brings together vehicle airflow, wind generation, energy storage and illumination.

Discipline
Energy & systems
My role
Team lead
Stage
Student concept
Focus
SDG 7 / Clean energy

01 / The idea

Start with
what moves.

The movement of vehicles suggests an engineering question: could some of the surrounding airflow contribute to a local lighting system?

Original concept illustration. It explains the system idea; it is not a photograph of a built or deployed prototype.

02 / The system

Follow the energy.

Explore each stage of the concept.
Every connection introduces a new constraint.

01 / Vehicle airflow

The starting question: could airflow generated by passing vehicles be captured as an input to a decentralized lighting concept? The available energy depends on real traffic and airflow conditions.

02 / Wind generation

The concept explores vertical-axis wind turbines as a way to convert moving air into rotation and electrical energy. This is a proposed system element, not a claim of measured roadside power.

03 / Energy storage

Storage is part of the system concept because generation and lighting demand do not necessarily happen at the same time. Capacity and losses would need to be evaluated together.

04 / Wireless transfer

A wireless energy transfer demonstration formed part of the project. This does not establish transfer efficiency, range or suitability for a deployed street-lighting system.

05 / Illumination

Fluorescent illumination and automatic light sensing complete the concept. Light sensing allows the lighting response to relate to ambient conditions.

Behind the concept

03

The engineering question.

A lighting system needs more than a source of motion. Generation, storage, transfer and demand must work together. The concept brings these elements into one system to examine their connections.

04

My role: connect the work.

I led the team developing LUMOS, bringing the sustainable-energy idea and its individual elements into a shared project direction.

05

Concept, with clear limits.

LUMOS is a student engineering and innovation project. The wireless transfer element was a demonstration. The project is not presented as a commercially deployed or independently validated urban lighting system.

06

The next questions.

How much energy could the airflow provide under measured conditions? What are the losses across the system? How would maintenance and storage affect practical usefulness?

These are questions for further evaluation, not claimed results.
